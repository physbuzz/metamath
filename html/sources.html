Sources

Inventory of src/book/dontshare, matched to actual passages in the study notes. Chapter numbers below refer to our notes, not Megill's book. “Listed” means a reading recommendation, not evidence that we used the source. “No use located” means no identifiable citation or passage; it does not establish that a source was never consulted. PDF/DjVu copies are counted once, with OCR companions grouped under the same work. Links are relative to the hosted sources.html; PDF page numbers count from the first page of the file, with printed page numbers noted separately.

Local copies and where they came up

  • Gaifman 2002, A Hilbert Type Deductive System for Sentential Logic, Completeness and Compactness. Ch04, §4.0.1 and quiz Q2: definitions of inference rule, premises/conclusion, and Hilbert-type deductive system; the discussion of whether Metamath itself should be called Hilbert-style. The definitions and modus ponens are on PDF p. 1 (printed p. 1). No use of the completeness/compactness proofs located.

  • Harper 2012, Practical Foundations for Programming Languages. Ch04, §§4.0.1–2: judgments, inductive definitions by rules, premise-free axioms versus proper rules, the tree example, and derived rules. Alpha-equivalence in peano.mm prompted the ch1–3 reading. Ch05 scratchwork, §5.0.2, uses ch1 for binding/capture; the ch5.5 reading chapter listed in the contents has no source file yet. Rules and the tree example: PDF pp. 34–35 (printed pp. 16–17); α-equivalence: PDF pp. 28–30 (printed pp. 10–12).

  • Buss 2023, Introduction to Mathematical Logic. Ch04, §4.0.1: PL-proofs, ⊢ versus ⊨, modus ponens notation, “axiom scheme,” and the quoted historical footnote on Hilbert-style systems. PDF pp. 55–56 (printed pp. 47–48), especially footnote 2 on p. 55 and Definitions II.1–2 on p. 56.

  • Mendelson, Introduction to Mathematical Logic, 6th ed. (2015). Ch04, §4.1.1: the id exercise is identified as Lemma 1.8: PDF pp. 54–55 (printed pp. 29–30 in this edition). Also listed among the primary sources. Broader propositional-calculus reading appears in old drafts, but is not evidence of further use in the current chapter.

  • Hirst & Hirst 2015, A Primer for Logic and Proof. Unused reading recommendation, acquired through metamath.org; primarily ch04, with ch01 as a secondary association. Listed in links; the ch04 §4.1.1 id comment mentions “our first proof,” but this inherited cross-reference does not mean we worked from the book. hirst/index.md and hirst/images/ are its OCR companions.

  • Tarski 1964, A Simplified Formalization of Predicate Logic with Identity. Ch05, §5.1: the predicate axioms, especially ax6v identified as B7 with distinct x, y: PDF p. 15 (printed p. 75). Explicitly listed as required reading in scratchwork, which also connects Tarski's approach to distinct variables; the dedicated reading notes are unfinished.

  • Megill 1995, A Finitely Axiomatized Formalization of Predicate Calculus with Equality. Ch05 scratchwork, “Required reading” / “Notes on Megill 1995”: listed alongside Tarski for the predicate-calculus work. The notes heading is empty; no specific result from this paper is cited in the current ch05 prose.

  • Van Heijenoort, ed., From Frege to Gödel. Covers two references in the ch05 introduction:

  • Skolem 1923, The foundations of elementary arithmetic established by means of the recursive mode of thought, without the use of apparent variables ranging over infinite domains: PDF pp. 313–344 (printed pp. 302–333). The editorial introduction starts on PDF p. 313; Skolem's text starts near the bottom of PDF p. 314, and his explicit quantifier-free program is on PDF p. 315 (printed p. 304).
  • Frege 1879, Begriffsschrift: §§11–12, PDF pp. 35–39 (printed pp. 24–28), on generality, scope, and combinations with negation. The closest passage to ch05's motivation for quantifiers is PDF p. 36 (printed p. 25): why the scope marker is necessary when generality occurs inside a judgment. The work's editorial introduction starts at PDF p. 12, printed p. 1; Frege's preface starts at PDF p. 16, printed p. 5. Ch05 still marks this reading as a TODO.

Both works are present in this volume, not missing PDFs.

  • Farmer, The Seven Virtues of Simple Type Theory (2008; rev. 2019). Ch04, §4.2.3: explicitly assigned reading for the hol.mm excursion and its distinction between |= judgments and object-language implication. No individual theorem from the paper is invoked.

  • Church 1940, A Formulation of the Simple Theory of Types. Ch04, §4.2.3: the other assigned reading for hol.mm; no individual result cited.

  • Pollack 1997, How to Believe a Machine-Checked Proof. Listed as essential reading in the introduction. An old draft (ai/05-logicians-rewrite.md), §5.1.4, explicitly attaches it to trusting the checker and writing independent checkers, referring back to ch03. That passage is not in the current chapters.

  • Barendregt & Wiedijk 2005, The Challenge of Computer Mathematics. Introduction, papers list: essential reading in the discussion of the de Bruijn criterion. Ch00 names the criterion but has an unfinished citation TODO; no more specific use of this paper located.

  • Kleene, Introduction to Metamathematics (1952; local 1971 copy, DjVu). Listed among the primary sources. An old AI reading list (ai/05-logicians.bak.md), §5.1, recommended §§19–23 for rule notation and formal deduction; that list explicitly says its section references were unchecked. No specific current-chapter use located.

  • Hamilton 1988, Logic for Mathematicians (DjVu). The ax-mp comment in the ch03 database cites Rule 1, p. 73: an inherited Metamath citation. The old reading list (ai/05-logicians.bak.md), §5.1, also recommended it for the three propositional axioms and idALT; no direct use in current chapter prose located.

  • Behnke et al., Fundamentals of Mathematics, vol. I (1974). A traceable connection is Megill's book, not a direct citation in our notes: §1.4.3 cites p. 6 on formalism; §4.5 cites p. 20 on theorems depending on definitions as well as axioms. Acquisition reason otherwise unconfirmed.

  • Frege, Basic Laws of Arithmetic, vol. I, Ebert–Rossberg translation draft. No direct use located. This is not the 1879 Begriffsschrift named in ch05; that work is in van Heijenoort above. frege/index.md and frege/images/ accompany this Basic Laws translation.

  • Benacerraf 1965, What Numbers Could Not Be. No citation or identifiable use located; acquisition reason unknown.

  • Ebbinghaus, Flum & Thomas, Mathematical Logic (local 2021 copy). No citation or identifiable use located; acquisition reason unknown.

  • Leary & Kristiansen 2015, A Friendly Introduction to Mathematical Logic, 2nd ed.. No citation or identifiable use located; acquisition reason unknown.

Referenced works without a copy in dontshare

These are gaps relative to the study notes, not an inventory of every reference inside the bundled Metamath book or downloaded .mm databases.

  • Peters 2022, Gödel's Theorem Without Tears. Directly used: ch05, §5.0.1, takes the arithmetic axioms from Definition 4.16, PDF p. 33 (printed p. 23); zos is Peters' CD.

  • Abelson & Sussman with Sussman, Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs. Directly used: ch02 and ch02.5, §4.4's query system: frames, matching/unification, rule search, and modifications to emit proof certificates.

  • Pavičić 1992, A New Axiomatization of Unified Quantum Logic. Ch04, §4.2.2, explicitly cites it when introducing ql.mm and ortholattices.

  • Barendregt 1997, The Impact of the Lambda Calculus in Logic and Computer Science. Introduction, papers list: cited specifically for coining “de Bruijn criterion.” Separate from the 2005 Barendregt–Wiedijk paper already present.

  • Wolfram, A New Kind of Science. Ch00, “An Undergraduate's Quest for Truth”: the Boolean-algebra/single-NAND-axiom discussion inspired the search over axiom systems. An autobiographical source, not a chapter exercise reference.
