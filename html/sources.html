Sources

The books and papers in book/dontshare, and where we used them. Page links use PDF page numbers; printed page numbers are given separately.

Local copies

  • Gaifman 2002, A Hilbert Type Deductive System for Sentential Logic, Completeness and Compactness. We used p. 1 for the definitions of inference rule and Hilbert-type deductive system in ch04 §4.0.1. This also prompted the discussion of whether Metamath itself should be called Hilbert-style. We haven't used the completeness or compactness proofs.

  • Harper 2012, Practical Foundations for Programming Languages. We used ch2 for judgments, inference rules, and the tree example in ch04 §4.0.1: PDF pp. 34–35 (printed pp. 16–17). Seeing “alpha-equivalence” in peano.mm was what sent us to ch1; that discussion is on PDF pp. 28–30 (printed pp. 10–12). Ch1–3 are also the reading for the planned ch5.5.

  • Buss 2023, Introduction to Mathematical Logic. We used his notation for proofs from hypotheses, ⊢ versus ⊨, and modus ponens in ch04 §4.0.1. PDF pp. 55–56 (printed pp. 47–48). The historical footnote we quote about Hilbert-style systems is footnote 2 on PDF p. 55.

  • Mendelson 2015, Introduction to Mathematical Logic, 6th ed.. Lemma 1.8 is the proof of idALT, mentioned with the id exercise in ch04 §4.1.1. PDF pp. 54–55 (printed pp. 29–30 in this edition; set.mm cites p. 36 of an older edition).

  • Hirst & Hirst 2015, A Primer for Logic and Proof. Downloaded because metamath.org recommended it, but we didn't end up using it. Mostly a ch04 reference, possibly ch01. “Our first proof,” PDF p. 23 (printed p. 17), is also idALT.

  • Hamilton 1988, Logic for Mathematicians. A book cited by set.mm for propositional calculus. Its Example 2.7(a), printed p. 31, is exactly the proof of idALT. Hamilton's book is shorter and gentler than Mendelson's. No direct use in current notes version. The local copy is DjVu.

  • Tarski 1964, A Simplified Formalization of Predicate Logic with Identity. The source of the predicate-calculus axioms we work through in ch05. In particular, our ax6v is his Axiom B7, with distinct variables x and y: PDF p. 15 (printed p. 75). Also on the ch05 reading list for understanding the treatment of variables.

  • Megill 1995, A Finitely Axiomatized Formalization of Predicate Calculus with Equality. Put on the ch05 reading list alongside Tarski. No direct use in current notes version; the reading notes are still empty.

  • Van Heijenoort, ed., From Frege to Gödel. Contains both historical sources we discuss in the ch05 introduction:

  • Skolem 1923, The foundations of elementary arithmetic established by means of the recursive mode of thought, without the use of apparent variables ranging over infinite domains. This is the reference for developing arithmetic without quantifiers. PDF pp. 313–344 (printed pp. 302–333), starting with the title and editorial introduction on p. 313. Skolem's text begins on PDF p. 314.

  • Frege 1879, Begriffsschrift. §§11–12 discuss generality and its scope: PDF pp. 35–39 (printed pp. 24–28). PDF p. 36 (printed p. 25) is the passage closest to our question about why quantifiers are needed inside statements. The chapter still marks this reading as a TODO. To read from the beginning, the editorial introduction starts at PDF p. 12, and Frege's preface at PDF p. 16.

  • Farmer 2008, The Seven Virtues of Simple Type Theory (rev. 2019). One of the two readings for the hol.mm section in ch04 §4.2.3. We don't cite a particular result from it.

  • Church 1940, A Formulation of the Simple Theory of Types. The other reading for ch04 §4.2.3, where we try hol.mm. We don't cite a particular result from it.

  • Pollack 1997, How to Believe a Machine-Checked Proof. On trusting a proof checker and checking proofs independently; especially PDF pp. 4–6 (printed pp. 2–4). Listed as essential reading in the introduction, alongside the discussion of trust in formal proofs.

  • Barendregt & Wiedijk 2005, The Challenge of Computer Mathematics. Listed in the introduction for the de Bruijn criterion: proofs should be checkable by a small independent program. The relevant discussion is on PDF p. 12 (also printed p. 12).

  • Kleene, Introduction to Metamathematics (1952; local 1971 copy). Listed among the primary sources. No direct use in current notes version. The local copy is DjVu.

  • Behnke et al. 1974, Fundamentals of Mathematics, vol. I. Megill cites printed p. 6 on formalism in his book's §1.4.3, and p. 20 on theorems depending on definitions as well as axioms in §4.5. No direct use in our notes.

  • Frege, Basic Laws of Arithmetic, vol. I, Ebert–Rossberg translation draft. No direct use in current notes version. This is a different work from the 1879 Begriffsschrift above.

  • Benacerraf 1965, What Numbers Could Not Be. No direct use in current notes version.

  • Ebbinghaus, Flum & Thomas, Mathematical Logic (local 2021 copy). No direct use in current notes version.

  • Leary & Kristiansen 2015, A Friendly Introduction to Mathematical Logic, 2nd ed.. No direct use in current notes version.

Referenced but missing from dontshare

  • Peters 2022, Gödel's Theorem Without Tears. We took the arithmetic axioms in ch05 §5.0.1 from Definition 4.16, PDF p. 33 (printed p. 23). Our zos is his axiom CD.

  • Abelson & Sussman with Sussman, Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs. §4.4 supplies the query system used in ch02 and ch02.5: matching, unification, and rule search. We modify it to produce Metamath proof certificates.

  • Pavičić 1992, A New Axiomatization of Unified Quantum Logic. Cited when introducing ql.mm and ortholattices in ch04 §4.2.2.

  • Barendregt 1997, The Impact of the Lambda Calculus in Logic and Computer Science. Cited in the introduction for coining “de Bruijn criterion.” This is a separate paper from Barendregt & Wiedijk 2005.

  • Wolfram, A New Kind of Science. In ch00, the discussion of Boolean algebra and a single NAND axiom inspired the idea of searching over axiom systems.
