5 Reading list

5.1 Read first

  • Mendelson, Introduction to Mathematical Logic, chapter 1, "The Propositional Calculus". (6th ed., CRC Press, 2015; the Metamath book cites the 2nd ed., 1979.) The chapter everyone points to. The section "An axiom system for the propositional calculus" defines formal theory, proof, and Γ ⊢ B, gives a three-axiom system L with modus ponens, proves B ⇒ B (Lemma 1.8, p. 36 (set.mm); this is idALT), then the deduction theorem and completeness. The next two sections cover independence via many-valued tables and other axiomatizations, including Łukasiewicz's and Meredith's. Two differences from set.mm: implication is written ⇒, and the third axiom is (¬C ⇒ ¬B) ⇒ ((¬C ⇒ B) ⇒ C) rather than ax-3.
  • Hamilton, Logic for Mathematicians, chapter 2, "Formal statement calculus". (Revised ed., Cambridge University Press, 1988.) The book set.mm and the Metamath book cite for propositional calculus. Its system L has exactly ax-1, ax-2, ax-3 (Definition 2.1, p. 28 (set.mm)), and its proof of A → A (Example 2.7(a), p. 31 (set.mm)) is idALT step for step. Shorter and gentler than Mendelson.
  • Kleene, Introduction to Metamathematics, §19–§23. (North-Holland, 1952.) The source for rule notation. §19 lists the postulates, with each rule of inference drawn as premises above a line and the conclusion below. §20 defines formal deduction and Γ ⊢ E. §21–§22 prove the deduction theorem. §23 collects derived rules, each stated as "if Γ ⊢ ... then Γ ⊢ ...". This is the closest thing to a definition of the rule figures that other books use without comment.

5.2 Rule notation and the turnstile

  • Harper, Practical Foundations for Programming Languages, chapter 2, "Inductive definitions". (2nd ed., Cambridge University Press, 2016.) Defines judgments and inference rules: premises above a line, conclusion below, and a derivation as a tree of rules. This is the rule figure, defined rather than assumed.
  • Megill and Wheeler, Metamath, Appendix C, "Metamath's formal system". (html, pdf.) Defines what a statement with $e hypotheses is, with no appeal to a logic textbook. The figure with ⊢ on every line, premises and conclusion alike, is this object.
  • ax-mp comment in set.mm. Says that the & and ⇒ in Proof Explorer displays abbreviate "and" and "implies" and are not part of the formal language. Cites Rule 1 of Hamilton p. 73 (set.mm).
  • Metamath Proof Explorer, "Deduction form and natural deduction" and "The weak deduction theorem". How set.mm stands in for Γ ⊢ φ (inference, deduction, and closed forms; the i/d suffixes).
  • Troelstra and Schwichtenberg, Basic Proof Theory, chapters 1–3. (2nd ed., Cambridge University Press, 2000.) Hilbert systems, natural deduction, and sequent calculi in one notation, with rule figures defined carefully.
  • Enderton, A Mathematical Introduction to Logic, §2.4, "A deductive calculus". (2nd ed., Academic Press, 2001.) Source of the Γ; α notation. Takes every tautology as an axiom, so its propositional proofs look nothing like chapter 04. Useful for ⊨ versus ⊢.
  • Frege, Begriffsschrift (1879). Origin of the turnstile (the judgment stroke joined to the content stroke). Notation only; not needed as reading.

5.3 The axioms in set.mm

  • Margaris, First Order Mathematical Logic. (Blaisdell, 1967; Dover reprint.) ax-1, ax-2, ax-3 are axioms A1–A3, p. 49 (set.mm); Theorem 1, p. 51 (set.mm) is idALT. The Metamath book cites it for the standard deduction theorem.
  • Hirst and Hirst, A Primer for Logic and Proof. (pdf.) Free. "Our first proof", p. 17 (set.mm), is idALT.
  • Church, Introduction to Mathematical Logic (Princeton University Press, 1956). System P₂ is the set.mm system. Heavy on history and attribution.
  • Whitehead and Russell, Principia Mathematica (1910–13). Source of the pm2.43-style labels: pm2.43 is theorem ∗2.43. Reference only.
  • Łukasiewicz and Tarski, "Untersuchungen über den Aussagenkalkül" (1930). English in Tarski, Logic, Semantics, Metamathematics. The three-axiom implication–negation systems, including luk-1–luk-3.
  • Meredith, "Single axioms for the systems (C,N), (C,O) and (A,N) of the two-valued propositional calculus", Journal of Computing Systems (1953), 155–164. The single axiom meredith.
  • Tarski, "A simplified formalization of predicate logic with identity", Archiv für Mathematische Logik und Grundlagenforschung 7 (1965), 61–79, and Megill, "A finitely axiomatized formalization of predicate calculus with equality", Notre Dame Journal of Formal Logic 36 (1995), 435–453. Predicate calculus without free-variable side conditions. Not needed until $d.

5.4 Metatheorems

  • Deduction theorem. Herbrand (1930 thesis) and Tarski (1930), independently. Read the proof in Mendelson chapter 1 or Kleene §21.
  • Completeness of propositional calculus. Post, "Introduction to a general theory of elementary propositions", American Journal of Mathematics 43 (1921), 163–185. Read the proof in Mendelson chapter 1 or Hamilton chapter 2 (the "adequacy theorem").
  • Admissible rules. Lorenzen, Einführung in die operative Logik und Mathematik (1955). Source of the admissible/derivable distinction.
  • MIU. Hofstadter, Gödel, Escher, Bach (1979), chapter 1; Metamath Appendix D; miu.mm. The Mathlib archive has a machine-checked proof that MU is not derivable.

5.5 Sequent calculus

  • Gentzen, "Untersuchungen über das logische Schließen", Mathematische Zeitschrift 39 (1934–35). English in Szabo (ed.), The Collected Papers of Gerhard Gentzen (1969). Natural deduction, sequent calculus, and cut elimination. Readable.
  • Negri and von Plato, Structural Proof Theory. (Cambridge University Press, 2001.) Textbook treatment of sequent calculus and cut elimination.
  • Jaśkowski, "On the rules of suppositions in formal logic" (1934). Natural deduction, independently of Gentzen.

5.6 History

  • Frege, Begriffsschrift (1879). First formal system in the modern sense. ax-1 is its Proposition 1, p. 26 (set.mm).
  • Hilbert and Ackermann, Grundzüge der theoretischen Logik (1928). The textbook behind the name "Hilbert system".
  • Gödel (1930, 1931). Completeness of predicate calculus; incompleteness.
  • Tarski, "The concept of truth in formalized languages" (1933). Object language and metalanguage.
  • Church (1936), Turing (1936). Definitions of "mechanical procedure".
  • Kleene (1952). Fixed much of the notation still in use.

5.7 Proof checkers

  • de Bruijn, Automath (1967 onward). Van Benthem Jutting checked Landau's Grundlagen der Analysis in it (thesis, 1977).
  • Pollack, "How to believe a machine-checked proof" (1998). In Twenty-Five Years of Constructive Type Theory. The de Bruijn criterion, argued carefully.
  • Shankar, Metamathematics, Machines, and Gödel's Proof. (Cambridge University Press, 1994; thesis 1986.) Incompleteness checked in the Boyer–Moore prover.
  • Harrison, "Towards self-verification of HOL Light" (IJCAR 2006).
  • Carneiro, "Models for Metamath" (2016, arXiv:1601.07699) and "Metamath Zero" (2019, arXiv:1910.10703). Metamath's formal system stated set-theoretically; a verifier designed to be proved correct.
